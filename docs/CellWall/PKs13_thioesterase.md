PKs-13 is an essential enzyme for the biosynthesis of mycolic acid and it is found in other mycobacteria, corynebacterial such as diphtheria, and also some other bacterial species. In addition this enzyme is not found in eukaryotes making it a promising target to fight against TB.



## Overall Pathway:

1. In the Mycolic acid biosynthesis pathway FAS-I produces C22-C26 fatty acids and FAS-II produces meromycolate chains which enzyme FadD32 then adds AMP forming meromycolate-AMP.

2\. Pks-13 then links the substrates from FAS-I and FAS-II

* Meromycolate-AMP binds in the ACP1 domain
* FAS-I substrate is carboxylated and then binds to the ACP2 domain
* PKs-13 then links both chains together through a Claisen-type condensation leading to the formation of an alpha-alkyl-beta-keto mycolic acid

3\. Pks-13 thioesterase domain then transfers new product from the ACP and links it to trehalose (sugar) which is then transported from the cytoplasm to periplasm by Mmpl3 enzyme which is then attached to the arabinogalactan.

&#x20;



An important domain in the enzyme is the thioesterase (TE) domain. Although the TE domain is found in other bacteria the specific mechanism in TB is distinct from the other bacteria. In TB the thioesterase domain is responsible for releasing the substrate and then trasfers it to trehalose to form the trehalose monomycolate which allows for the mycolic acid to then bind with the arabinogalactan and peptidoglycan layer.



## Protein and natural substrate

Pks-13 contains 5 domains:

* ACP1 - activated meromycolates bind to
* ACP2 - Binds the FAS-II substrate
* Thioesterase (TE) - binds the alpha-alkyl-beta-ketoacyl product and then transfers it to trehalose.

## Potential Inhibitors

Currently there are no approved inhibitors, however several are in preclinical trials or experimental development.  Notable inhibitors include benzofurans (e.g. TAM-16) and coumestans (coumestan 8). One of the major issues that arises from these inhibitors is their high toxicity levels which render them unable to be used given that a person is unable to take the inhibitor without resulting in addition health problems. Currently research is focused on altering the structure of certain inhibitors in order to reduce their toxic levels but still have the same potency to be able to inhibit the thioesterase domain.



Most inhibitors interact with active site residues: Ser1533, Asp1560, and His1699



## Key interactions

A common occurrence in most of the inhibitors is there interaction with the enzyme's active site specifically the areas Ser1533, Asp1560, and His1699
